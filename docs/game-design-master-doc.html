Partisan Project

Escape from Tarkov meets Mount and Blade with guns

Inspired by the Antistasi mod for Arma 3. The basic idea is a PvE loop much like SP Tarkov. Instead of looting guns just for yourself, however, you play as a group of units and level up your characters and your Resistance through a Mount and Blade style overworld game. This makes gun and attachment collecting mean something at a gameplay level: stealing weapons lets you level up different characters and expand your Resistance.

The metagame then escalates from small engagements, ambushes and supply raids to all-out warfare with vehicles, as you finally drive the Invader off your island.

Doctrine

Four pillars

Gear is identity

You are what you carry. Everything wearable or mountable is a slot with options, so a hundred loadouts come from a dozen parts.

Readable at a glance

Low-poly, faceted, flat-shaded. A silhouette must tell you who it is: hood, helmet and tubes, beanie, pack, rifle outline.

Smart because it speaks

Perceived intelligence comes from communication, not computation: the enemy's decisions must be audible and legible.

Fair, not cheap

Difficulty scales what the enemy knows and how fast it learns, never its health, damage or reflexes. Built for players who grew out of twitch.

Tone

Low poly and loud

Partisan uses a native three.js low-poly art style, but its animation and sound are inspired by hardcore milsim. Gunfights are loud and chaotic.

Design

Core loop, progression and features

Provisional Setting canon (WP-D1) is undecided, so fiction-dependent details below are placeholders. Mechanics follow the pillars and the AI compendium.

Core loop

  1. Prepare: build the loadout and operator in the customisers.
  2. Infiltrate: move through a small contested area, reading radio chatter and enemy calls.
  3. Engage: short, loud, chaotic fights where the enemy acts on what it believes it knows.
  4. Withdraw: leave with what you carried out; losses and finds feed the next preparation.

Progression

Progress is access, not power: new slots and options unlock, while enemy knowledge and learning speed scale difficulty (pillar 4). Unlock order and economy are provisional.

AI in one paragraph

Enemies hold beliefs about the player (last seen, likely routes, noise heard), act on them, and say what they are doing. Difficulty changes belief quality and learning rate, never health or aim. Full reasoning and the audience study: AI design compendium.

Art direction

Moodboard

Faceted low poly with painterly facets, desaturated olive and coyote, and light that does the storytelling.

2D sprite ideas

  • Thick black outline around every shape: the figure reads against any ground, from any height.
  • Chunky, exaggerated gear (pack, helmet, kneepads): the role shows in the silhouette, the same rule as the 3-D operators.
  • Few flat tones per surface with a darker shade: it is close to the Art Style Lab's cel and ink looks, so we can test both before choosing.
  • A real rifle shape held across the body: the weapon family stays recognisable at small sizes.
  • Candidate look for the top-down shooter's rebels and soldiers.

2.5-D graphics direction

  • The shooter becomes RimWorld-style 2.5-D top-down sprites: thick dark outlines, flat fills with a shade, layered people, long sun shadows. The simulation does not change.
  • The campaign map becomes a retro island map like a handheld-RPG region map, with territory shown by tint and by shape.
  • The plan is in the graphics roadmap (WP-V1 to V20).

Rules of the look

  1. Faceted, flat-shaded. Visible polygons on skin, cloth and armour; colour varies per facet, never a smooth normal map.
  2. Silhouette first. Recognisable from a black cut-out at hero distance.
  3. Layered identity. Slots, colour zones and insignia over one skeleton.
  4. Palette discipline. Muted kit, saturated rewards: a patch, a carabiner, a spark.
  5. Two lights. Golden-hour dust, or overcast haze with god-rays; rim light on dark kit.
  6. Storytelling props. Casings, rebar, worn paint, taped magazines, a radio.
  7. Real silhouettes for weapons, with modern furniture on historic bodies.
  8. Hero pose language. Weight on one leg, rifle upright or low, head turned toward camera, camera 24° off front.
  9. The UI is a phone. 1-bit LCD tones, ordered dither, stencil type, hard edges.

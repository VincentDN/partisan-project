PARTISAN

Yantis, autumn 2012. The last Ottoman island.

Greece has been free for a hundred and eighty years. Yantis has not. When the garrison's relief column fails to sail, a handful of islanders with hunting rifles and a fishing fleet decide to stop waiting for Athens and take the island themselves.

What this is

The one-paragraph version, for anyone arriving cold.

PARTISAN is a WW2044 project. Not a spin-off. It sits on the same timeline, one shift earlier.

WW2044 runs the twentieth century a hundred years late: the Second World War is fought in the 2040s with the hardware of the 2040s, but with the doctrine, alliances and stakes of 1939 to 1945. Crete is still the turning point. It just happens in 2041.

Apply the same shift to the Balkan Wars and 1912 becomes 2012. That is where PARTISAN lives. The Ottoman Empire is still standing, still holding the last of the eastern Aegean, and still two years away from the war that will end it. The island of Yantis rises before the Greek fleet arrives, runs itself for five months, and is then annexed by the country it was fighting to join.

The men who hold the ridge above Yantis harbour in 2012 are the grandfathers of the men who hold Crete in 2041. That is the whole reason this project exists.

Historical anchor. This is the Free State of Icaria, July to November 1912. Ikarian islanders threw out their Ottoman garrison on their own initiative, months before the First Balkan War formally began, and ran an independent state with its own flag, stamps and militia until Greece annexed it. Yantis is that story with the serial numbers filed off and a hundred years added.

Where it sits

The WW2044 ladder holds all the way down. Every rung is its historical counterpart plus one hundred years.

  1. 2011 ← 1911

    The empire is overstretched

    An Italian war in North Africa pulls Ottoman reserves south and leaves the Aegean garrisons thin, unpaid and resented. Yantis has a company where it should have a battalion.

  2. 2012 ← 1912 · PARTISAN

    The island rises

    The uprising, the five-month republic, the arrival of the Hellenic Navy, and the annexation nobody on the island voted for. This is the playable window.

  3. 2013 ← 1913

    The victors turn on each other

    The Balkan allies fall out over the spoils. Yantis is now Greek territory and the partisans are now, on paper, Greek soldiers. Several of them object.

  4. 2014 ← 1914

    The first great war

    The Balkan question detonates. The Ottoman Empire enters and does not leave. Yantis becomes a rear-area supply anchorage and stays one for four years.

  5. 2041 ← 1941

    Crete

    The WW2044 turning point. Airborne insertion at night, the doctrine the setting is built around. Yantis veterans, or their grandsons, are on the receiving end.

  6. 2044 ← 1944

    WW2044

    The parent project. PARTISAN is its prologue, thirty-two years and one technological generation earlier.

The thirty-two year gap is the feature, not the problem. It buys a completely different silhouette, a completely different weapon set and a completely different visual language from WW2044, while staying inside the same continuity.

The island

Terrain first. Everything about how this plays comes out of the ground.

Yantis is a long, thin, steep eastern Aegean island: a limestone spine running most of its length, terraced olive and vine on the south slopes, pine and scrub on the north, and almost no flat ground. One deep harbour town. A handful of inland villages reachable only by switchback. A single coast road that can be cut in four places by two men and a crowbar.

That geography does the design work. The garrison holds the harbour, the customs house and the road. The partisans hold everything above the treeline and everything the road cannot reach. Neither can dislodge the other without help from the sea, which is why the sea is where the campaign is decided.

The island is close enough to the Anatolian coast to see it on a clear day. That sightline is the whole strategic problem in one image.

Anchor locations

  • The harbour town, customs house and Ottoman barracks
  • The coast road and its four cut points
  • The monastery on the spine, the partisans' first headquarters
  • The abandoned emery workings, arms cache and prison
  • The fishing anchorages on the north shore, the smuggling route
  • The signal station on the summit, contested throughout

Names deliberately withheld. Yantis does not currently scan as a Greek toponym and the whole naming layer should be settled in one pass rather than accreted.

Three sides, not two

The third faction is the point. The relief force is not straightforwardly the cavalry.

GREF

The Yantis militia

Fishermen, quarrymen, shepherds, two ex-army sergeants and a schoolteacher who can write a proclamation. They liberated the island themselves and they have no intention of handing it to anyone.

Strength
Terrain knowledge, the fishing fleet, total local support
Weakness
No artillery, no medical chain, ammunition measured in rounds not crates
Wants
An independent Yantis, or failing that, terms
REDFOR

The garrison

An understrength company a long way from home, six months behind on pay, holding a town that hates them for a government that has stopped answering. Not villains. Men who want to go home and are being shot at by farmers.

Strength
The harbour, the only artillery on the island, actual training
Weakness
Cannot leave the road, cannot be reinforced, cannot trust the town
Wants
Relief, or a ship, or an honourable surrender
BLUFOR

The Hellenic relief force

Regular infantry landed off a cruiser, arriving after the hard part is done to accept a surrender the militia already secured. Competent, well equipped, and carrying annexation orders in a sealed envelope.

Strength
Naval gunfire, supply, legitimacy, numbers
Weakness
Knows nothing of the island, and is not entirely welcome
Wants
Yantis as Greek territory, with or without Yantine consent

The turn. The campaign should not end when the garrison surrenders. It should end when the militia has to decide what to do about the people who came to save them. That is the real Icaria story and it is worth more than another beach landing.

The constraint sheet

The single rule that makes the setting legible, and what it lets in and keeps out.

The rule: real hardware of the stated year, historical war of the year minus one hundred. WW2044 is 2040s equipment fighting 1940s. PARTISAN is 2010s equipment fighting 1910s. Anything that fails that test is out, however good it looks.

Applies to art, models, sound, UI and copy alike.
LayerInOut
Small arms Kalashnikov pattern, worn G3s, hunting shotguns, bolt guns older than the men holding them Anything from the WW2044 kit sheet, modular rails on militia weapons
Optics and night Cheap red dots on a minority of weapons, handheld torches, flares, one prized monocular Quad-tube night vision, thermal, the 2044 nocturnal doctrine
Vehicles Pickups, an ex-municipal truck, mules, caiques, one requisitioned ferry Armour of any kind on the island. The road will not take it.
Comms Mobile phones with intermittent coverage, handheld VHF off the boats, runners, church bells Encrypted mesh, drones, satellite links, anything that removes uncertainty
Uniform Civilian clothes with an armband, mismatched surplus, chest rigs over fishing jumpers A partisan uniform. There isn't one. That is the visual identity.
Garrison kit Genuine issued uniform, correct but old, visibly unmaintained and unreplaced Modern professional-army silhouette. They are a neglected colonial garrison.

Production note. This constraint set is cheap. 2012 irregular warfare is the most photographed conflict aesthetic of the last two decades, so reference is everywhere and the asset burden is a fraction of the bespoke 2044 hardware. That is a real argument for making PARTISAN the next thing rather than the someday thing.

Design pillars

Four. If a decision does not serve one of these, it does not go in.

The ground is the enemy

No flat ground, one road, and a ridgeline nobody can hold cheaply. Movement should cost more than shooting does.

Scarcity over loadout

Ammunition, medicine and boats are the resources. There is no armoury. What the militia carries is what the militia found.

Nobody here is a monster

The garrison is unpaid and abandoned. The relief force is not lying about its orders. The tragedy works only if all three sides are reasonable.

Liberation is not the end

The island wins and then loses itself. The second half of the campaign is political, and it should hurt more than the first.

Open questions

Unresolved. Listed here so they stop being re-litigated in chat.

  • Is Yantis the name? It does not read as Greek. Decide the whole toponym layer at once, island and locations together, before any art is labelled.
  • Does the Ottoman Empire survive as the Ottoman Empire? A straight hundred-year shift keeps the empire alive to 2012, which is the cleanest option and matches the WW2044 method. The alternative is a modern successor state that is Ottoman in name and posture. Pick one and hold it, because the flags and insignia fall out of this answer.
  • How much does 2012 real-world politics show through? 2012 already carries a heavy set of associations for irregular Mediterranean warfare. Deliberate resonance or careful distance, but not accident.
  • Does PARTISAN get its own domain? American Kingdoms and The Divided States both did. WW2044 is still a Shopify page. This site currently assumes hidden and internal, and takes no position.
  • Is the playable window the uprising, the republic, or the annexation? All three is the ambitious answer. The republic is the one nobody else has made.
  • Wiki or no wiki? AK runs collaborative lore on Miraheze. A single island with a five-month history may be better served closed and authored.
